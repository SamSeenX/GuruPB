While the specific topics change with every economic cycle, the underlying architecture of an info-product funnel has remained largely identical for over thirty years.

## Stage 1: The Visual Bait

The funnel starts with status intimidation designed to bypass critical thinking:

- Rented high-end properties, luxury vehicles, and private aviation backdrops.
- Narrative framing pitting the viewer into a stark dilemma: remain an overworked corporate wage earner or gain complete sovereign autonomy.
- The promise of asymmetrical leverage, suggesting that complex business outcomes require only a laptop and a straightforward copy-paste system.

## Stage 2: The Shovel (The Core Product)

The guru does not make their primary fortune running the business they advocate. Instead, they monetize the instruction manual:

- Basic, publicly available information is assembled into branded video courses.
- The curriculum is labeled as proprietary blueprints, secret methods, or closed-door frameworks.
- Access to private community forums or chat servers is positioned as high-value networking.

## Stage 3: The Micro-Upsell and Backend Ladder

The initial transaction is merely an qualification filter for high-ticket extractions:

- Low-ticket introductory guides ($10 to $47) or free live bootcamps filter for credit card readiness.
- Checkout flows present immediate order bumps and one-click upgrades.
- The real financial target is the high-ticket mastermind, priced between $2,000 and $25,000 per seat.

## Stage 4: Deflection of Blame

The definitive feature of the guru model is total liability transfer:

- Contractual terms classify all purchases as educational materials with strict zero-refund policies.
- When customers fail to generate income, the guru claims the failure was purely internal due to poor execution, lacking work ethic, or incorrect mindset.
- This psychological loop preserves the illusion of the system while silencing legitimate consumer criticism.
