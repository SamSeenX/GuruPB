Online business educators rarely stay anchored to a single trade. A structural examination of the industry reveals an inevitable 18 to 24 month expiration window for every hyped business model.

## The Problem of Product Exhaustion

Every info-product faces a natural lifecycle:

1. **The Pioneer Window**: Early adopters make real profits before the strategy becomes common knowledge.
2. **The Guru Wave**: Educators create courses claiming anyone can replicate the results without experience.
3. **Market Saturation**: Thousands of students flood the niche using identical outreach scripts and funnels.
4. **Diminishing Returns & Negative Reviews**: Conversion rates crash, buyers express dissatisfaction, and online search results fill with warnings.

## Historical Precedents

This pattern predates the commercial internet:

- **Don Lapre (1990s)**: Built an infomercial empire selling classified ad kits. When newspapers declined, he launched a massive multi-level vitamin marketing venture before facing federal wire and mail fraud charges.
- **Carlton Sheets (1980s - 2000s)**: Marketed cassette tapes on no-down-payment real estate investing, cycling through different economic climates until regulatory scrutiny increased.
- **Modern Digital Era**: Gurus transitioned rapidly from dropshipping and affiliate blogging in 2016, to SMMA in 2018, crypto and Web3 in 2021, and generative AI tools today.

## The Invariant Truth

Technologies evolve, but the core business of selling aspirational shortcuts to financially anxious audiences remains constant. Recognizing the pivot cycle allows consumers to assess promotional claims with skepticism before committing capital.
